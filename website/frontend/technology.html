Algorithm

1. CACCS Scheduling Algorithm

The Cost-Aware Constrained Charging Scheduler (CACCS) is a greedy heuristic algorithm developed by Team 12 at Eindhoven University of Technology. It minimises the total charging cost over a plug-in window while jointly penalising grid overload — a problem that naive price-only approaches ignore.

1.1 Optimisation Objective

CACCS minimises the following scalar cost functional over the plug-in horizon H = {plugin_hour, …, deadline_hour − 1}:

J = Σh∈H [ α · ph · xh / η + β · max(0, Lh + xh/Pmax − Lcap)² ]

Subject to: Σh∈H xh = E (energy target), 0 ≤ xh ≤ Pmax for all h.

  • ph — Retail electricity price at hour h (€/kWh), from Eneco or Essent dynamic tariff
  • xh — Energy allocated to hour h (kWh) — the decision variable
  • η — Charger efficiency, default 0.92 (IEC 62196 AC Level 2 standard)
  • Lh — Normalised grid load at hour h, derived from the Eindhoven ElaadNL proxy (range 0–1)
  • β — Grid congestion penalty weight, user-tunable from 0 (price-only) to 1 (maximum grid relief)
  • Lcap — Grid capacity threshold, fixed at 0.90 (90% of substation rating)
  • α — Cost weight, set to 1 − β. At β = 0, CACCS reduces to pure price optimisation.

1.2 Greedy Algorithm

CACCS solves the optimisation iteratively with a 0.25 kWh step size Δ. At each iteration, it evaluates the marginal cost of adding Δ kWh to each available hour h in the window:

ΔJh = α · ph · Δ / η + β · [max(0, Lh + (xh+Δ)/Pmax − Lcap)² − max(0, Lh + xh/Pmax − Lcap)²]

It assigns Δ to the hour with the minimum ΔJh, increments xh, and repeats until the total energy target E is reached. This greedy approach converges in E/Δ ≤ 400 iterations for a 100 kWh target — completing in under 1 ms on any modern device.

Pricing

2. Dutch Electricity Prices & Retail Formula

SmartEV uses the dynamic tariffs of Eneco Dynamische Stroom and Essent Dynamisch. Both suppliers pass through the EPEX SPOT Day-Ahead price — settled hourly at the NL bidding zone — plus a fixed margin and the mandatory Dutch energy tax (energiebelasting).

2.1 Retail Price Formula

Retail (€/kWh) = (Wholesale (€/MWh) ÷ 1000 + Supplier Margin + NL_Tax) × (1 + VAT)

Eneco: (Wholesale/1000 + 0.022 + 0.10154) × 1.21
Essent: (Wholesale/1000 + 0.018 + 0.10154) × 1.21

2.2 Price Components (2026)

  • EPEX Wholesale — Day-Ahead auction, hourly, €/MWh. NL 2024 annual mean: €84.7/MWh
  • Supplier margin — Eneco €0.022/kWh · Essent €0.018/kWh (from published tariff sheets)
  • Energiebelasting (EB) — Fixed at €0.10154/kWh (Belastingdienst 2026 tariff tier 1)
  • BTW (VAT) — 21% applied to the sum above

Regulation

3. ERE Certificates & NEa Methodology

ERE (Energiereductie-Erkend) certificates are the Dutch implementation of the EU Renewable Energy Directive RED III (EU 2023/2413), Article 22a on renewable fuels of non-biological origin. They are issued by the Nederlandse Emissieautoriteit (NEa) from 1 January 2026.

When you charge your EV at home with a Dutch grid connection, a portion of that energy is provably renewable (the grid mix). Each kWh generates a fractional ERE certificate, which has monetary value in the compliance market.

3.1 ERE Yield Calculation

ERE / kWh = NL_Renewable_Share × CO₂_Emission_Factor × MJ_per_kWh ÷ 1000

= 0.505 × 183 gCO₂eq/kWh × 3.6 MJ/kWh ÷ 1000

= 0.333 ERE per kWh charged
  • NL_Renewable_Share = 0.505 — The fraction of Dutch grid electricity from renewable sources (CBS, 2024 provisional)
  • CO₂ factor = 183 gCO₂eq/kWh — NL grid-average emission intensity (RVO, 2025 methodology)
  • 3.6 MJ/kWh — Energy conversion constant (1 kWh = 3.6 MJ)
  • Division by 1,000 converts grams to kg, aligning with NEa unit definition

Solar Energy

4. Solar Self-Consumption & Post-Saldering

Until 2027, the Dutch saldering (net metering) scheme allowed residential solar owners to offset exported electricity 1:1 against consumption, making feed-in as valuable as self-use. This scheme ends on 1 January 2027.

From 2027, excess solar is reimbursed at the low wholesale feed-in rate (typically ~€0.06–0.08/kWh), while you still pay full retail price (~€0.20–0.30/kWh) for grid imports. The value gap is €0.12–0.22 per kWh — making self-consumption critically important.

4.1 Self-Consumption Value

Value per matched kWh = Retail price − Feed-in rate

≈ €0.23 − €0.07 = €0.16 extra per kWh self-consumed

4.2 Smart vs Passive Matching Rates

  • Passive rate (30%) — Random overlap between daytime solar generation and uncontrolled EV charging
  • CACCS smart rate (70%) — EV charging deliberately shifted to the solar peak window (typically 10:00–14:00 on sunny days)
  • Delta = +40% × annual solar yield = additional matched kWh per year

Infrastructure

5. Dutch Grid Congestion (Netcongestie)

Grid congestion is one of the most acute infrastructure challenges facing the Netherlands. The national grid operator TenneT and regional DSOs (Enexis, Stedin, Liander) have all declared formal congestion in large parts of the country.

"Noord-Brabant's demand-side headroom is negative 14%: 3,106 MW of requested transport capacity versus 2,718 MW available. Every kilowatt-hour of EV charging moved out of peak hours buys grid headroom."

TenneT TSO B.V. (2025) — Transport- en aansluitcapaciteit hoogspanningsnet

5.1 The Evening Peak Problem

More than 60% of MV/LV distribution substations in Eindhoven regularly exceed 90% of rated capacity during the 17:00–20:00 evening window. This is driven by the coincidence of:

  • Residential EV charging starting as drivers return home (~18h)
  • Electric heat pumps reaching peak demand as temperatures drop (~19h)
  • Near-zero solar generation after sunset
  • Industrial load not yet at overnight minimum

5.2 CACCS Congestion Penalty

Penalty at hour h = β × max(0, Lh + xh/Pmax − 0.90)²

Activates only when effective grid load exceeds 90% of substation capacity.
